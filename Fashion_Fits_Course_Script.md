# Fashion Fits: Udemy Course Video Script

Instructor narration for each slide. Total runtime about 16 minutes. Bracketed cues are pauses or actions.

## Slide 2: What you will learn (about 1 min)

Before we dive in, let me show you what you'll walk away with.

You'll learn how to prove a real problem using research and a story. You'll learn how to pick a niche and own it, so you're not competing with everyone. You'll design an AI feature step by step, build it responsibly, and finally pitch it in ten minutes.

There are six sections, about three hours of video, and one final project. Every section ends with a short exercise, so you're building as you learn, not just watching.

## Slide 3: Roadmap (about 1 min)

Here's your roadmap.

Section one is this welcome and the project overview. Section two, we find and prove the problem. Section three, we choose our niche. Section four is the exciting one: we design the solution. Section five covers responsible AI and how we stand apart from competitors. And section six, we put it all together into a pitch.

Notice the last circle is highlighted. Everything we do leads to that final pitch, so keep it in mind as we go.

## Slide 4: The Problem (about 2 min)

Every great pitch starts with a problem people feel. So let's look at ours.

Our research suggests around 70 percent of online clothing returns are due to sizing issues. That's the single biggest reason people send clothes back.

Why? Because a size label doesn't mean what we think it means. A size 12 in Australia is not a size 12 in the United States, or in Japan. And a vintage size 12 from the 1980s is different again.

[Pause]

Now, look at who gets hurt. The customer wastes money, pays for return shipping and loses time. The business pays shipping and handling costs, has stock tied up, and loses something harder to measure: trust. When a customer is disappointed once, they hesitate to come back.

Your exercise: draw a pain map with two columns, customer and business. List every cost you can think of.

## Slide 5: Why second-hand? (about 1.5 min)

You might ask: why second-hand? Why not all online fashion?

Because second-hand has this problem in its most extreme form. At a mainstream retailer, you can order two sizes and return the one that doesn't fit. In second-hand, that option doesn't exist. There's one jacket, one dress, one pair of jeans. If it doesn't fit, that's it.

On top of that, many sellers are everyday people. They don't measure their items, tags are faded or in another country's sizing system, or the tag is missing altogether.

And here's the sustainability angle. When a wrong-size purchase ends up unused or in the bin, the environmental benefit of buying second-hand disappears. Demand is growing every year. What's missing is confidence.

Quick quiz after this video: why is second-hand the most extreme version of the sizing problem?

## Slide 6: The solution in four steps (about 2.5 min)

Now let's design the solution. Meet Fashion Fits, an AI feature that sits inside the online marketplace, so the customer never has to download anything.

Step one, the tag. The seller or buyer takes a simple photo of the clothing tag. No tape measure, no forms.

Step two, the AI reads it. It picks out the brand, size, country and fabric, finds the brand's published size information, and works out what that label means in real measurements.

Step three, the match. It compares those measurements with the customer's own size profile.

Step four, the score. Every item gets a fit percentage, like 92 percent match for you.

[Pause]

Your hands-on task: sketch these four screens in Canva or Figma. Keep them simple. Clear beats fancy.

## Slide 7: The hero feature (about 1 min)

Let's zoom in on the part I'm most excited about: the fit percentage.

Fashion Fits doesn't just say "this might fit." It gives a number, so the customer knows exactly how confident the recommendation is.

Picture it. You're browsing, and next to every item is a number telling you how well it will fit you. No guessing. No disappointment. No wardrobe graveyard.

That's the key lesson for your own projects: turn hope into information. When your product gives people something clear to act on, that's when it becomes valuable.

## Slide 8: Responsible AI (about 2 min)

Any AI product needs to be built responsibly. We use four principles, and I want you to write your own by the end of this section.

First, privacy. We only photograph the clothing tag, never a person, and never a body scan.

Second, honesty. If the tag is unreadable or the brand information can't be found, the tool says so, lowers the confidence score and suggests checking with the seller. We'd rather admit uncertainty than give a wrong answer.

Third, respect. Our language is about the garment, not the body. We say "this jacket runs narrow," never "you are too big."

Fourth, human oversight. Sellers can correct the information, and those corrections make the tool better for everyone.

Exercise: write four responsible-AI principles for your own product.

## Slide 9: The market and our advantage (about 1.5 min)

Let's talk competition. Every good pitch acknowledges it.

Our main competitors are Garment DNA and True Fit. They're capable tools, but they try to serve everyone, including mainstream retailers, and second-hand is an adaptation.

Fashion Fits serves one group, and serves it deeply. We're built only for second-hand, where every item is unique and where reducing landfill matters most.

And look at the chain at the bottom. Satisfied customers build trust. Trust brings people back and attracts new ones. More customers means growth for the businesses that use us.

Takeaway: a specialist beats a generalist when the problem is specific.

## Slide 10: Structuring the pitch (about 2 min)

Now, let's turn all of this into a ten-minute pitch.

This chart shows how the time is split: one minute for the opening, two for the problem, a minute and a half on why second-hand, two and a half on the solution, then responsible AI, the market, next steps and a short close.

Three delivery tips. Pace yourself at about 140 to 150 words a minute. Use pauses, especially after a big question or a key number. And if you're presenting as a team, practise your handovers so they feel smooth.

Finish with your next step. For us, that's a pilot with a small second-hand platform, measuring fewer wrong-size purchases, fewer returns and happier customers.
